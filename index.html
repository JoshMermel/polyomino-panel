How to play

Goal

Draw along the dotted lines to cut all the white cells into tiles of the shape shown above the board. Every white cell belongs to exactly one tile, and tiles may be rotated or flipped. Black cells are not part of any tile. When the puzzle is solved the frame turns green.

Marking the board

  • Click or tap an edge to cycle it: line, then small ✕ ("no line here"), then empty.
  • Drag along edges to paint: every edge you cross becomes whatever the first one just became.
  • Drag across cells to trace a whole tile. If the cells you cover form the tile shape (green preview), letting go draws its outline and erases any lines already inside it.
  • Right-click an edge to toggle a ✕ directly.
  • Undo / Redo with the buttons or Ctrl/⌘-Z and Ctrl/⌘-Shift-Z. Esc cancels a drag in progress. Reset clears the board (and can be undone).

Checking and hints

  • Correct so far? tells you whether the lines and ✕ you've placed agree with the solution. It doesn't highlight anything, and lines you haven't drawn yet don't count against you.
  • Hint never changes your marks. It shows, in order of preference: marks that are wrong (red); a cell that only one tile can cover; lines and ✕ that every possible tile for a cell agrees on; a cell where all but one possible tile would lead to a contradiction (a cell with nowhere to go, or a walled-off pocket that can't be tiled); the same after following a chain of forced steps; or an edge that can take a ✕. Dashed lines show what to draw, ✕ marks what to cross, and yellow marks the cell(s) the hint is about. Press Hint again while one is showing to see the next hint of the same kind.

Other

  • Closed regions are shaded blue when they are a valid tile and red when they can't become one (turn this off in Settings ⚙).
  • Browse shows every puzzle in the book, grouped by difficulty, with the ones you've solved (green) and started (yellow); tap one to jump to it.
  • Your progress is saved in this browser for each puzzle.

Feedback

This is an early prototype. Too hard, too easy, a bug, a hint that didn't make sense? Report a problem or send a comment (the link fills in which puzzle you were on).

About

Based on L-panel, invented by Naoki Inaba, as presented in Alex Bellos' Puzzle Ninja, and generalized to other tile shapes. Every puzzle here is generated by this program and has exactly one solution.
