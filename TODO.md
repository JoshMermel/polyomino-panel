## Ideas for more experiments

- **Shapes book follow-ups.** `bonus-shapes` has 400 puzzles on 16 board shapes with 20 tile specs (12 single tiles, 8 pairs). Ideas: print shaped boards without the outside cells, more outlines (a heart, a cross with arms of unequal length, a hexagon), sparser puzzles (extra blocked cells start at about 6% of the open cells), three-shape puzzles, a pool-based pipeline like the main books.

- **Triangle grid.** The topology layer (`js/topology.js`, README "Grid topologies") was written so a triangle grid plugs in: `dirsAt(r, c)` by up/down parity (3 neighbours), 12 symmetries, a polyiamond catalogue (1, 1, 1, 3, 4, 12 shapes of 1 to 6 cells), polygon geometry. Open work: `boardPlacements` and the generator's random packing must only translate a tile by offsets that keep the up/down pattern; an `outside`-cut board shape (a big triangle or a hexagon of triangles); hit testing for small cells on a phone.
- **Hex follow-ups.** Pairs of hex tiles, hexagon boards of side 7, other outlines (a rhombus, a honeycomb "rectangle"), a parity-like rule for the 3-colouring of hexes, hex tiles in the Print booklet (drawn, but only checked by eye).
- **PDF of the shapes book.** Cells that are not part of a shaped board (the corners of a plus sign or an octagon, a donut's hole) are probably printed black, like blocked cells, instead of being left out; the app skips them (the `outside` column) but the square branch of `boardSvg` in `js/print.js` does not. (Hex boards already skip them and crop to the shape.) Check, then draw only the in-shape cells and a frame that follows the outline.
- **Denser printing.** Rethink the puzzles per page (1, 2, 4 or 6) and answer-key boards per page (`KEY_PER_PAGE`, 6) limits when printing to PDF: pages could carry more (8 or 9 puzzles, 12 or more small answers) and save paper. Look at the smallest board size that stays playable on paper, and let the choice depend on the board's size.
