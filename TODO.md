# To do

Ideas that are wanted but not scheduled.

- **Daily puzzle.** One puzzle per day picked from across shapes, with the difficulty varying by weekday (as in multiverse star battle). Gives friends a reason to come back.
- **More two-shape puzzles.** Every pair of shapes now has a pool (`tools/overnight.mjs`) and the pair books draw on them. Ideas: three or more shapes (triples generate fine but are untested through the fill, hunt and builder tools), and puzzles that *must* use each shape a given number of times.  Some domino pairs found no unique puzzle on larger boards (27 of 513 pair/size combinations are dead). If friends find the pair books too hard, raise `tierWeights.Beginner` in `tools/books/collections.json`. About a quarter of the Hard pair puzzles are "no logical hints" ones (beyond the hint rules), so the pair books get tough at the end.
- **Rectangular boards, trim wasted black edges.** A surprisingly large number of boards have a whole row or column (sometimes two edges) of black squares. Make the player handle rectangular boards (the generator and most of the code already take separate rows and columns) and edit those puzzles to remove the empty border rows/columns. Things to sort out: the puzzle hash includes the grid, so saved progress for trimmed puzzles would restart; books group puzzles by board size, which should become the trimmed size (or area); the solution string and rating don't change (blocked border cells play no part).
- **Guess layer (a checkpoint), for solving by short guess chains.** After a failed guess there can be 10 to 40 marks to erase, and Undo takes a tap per step. About 14% of the published puzzles need chains or more, and roughly a quarter to a third need a trial of some kind, mostly in the Hard books. Idea: a **Guess** toggle next to Undo starts a tentative layer (like using a different pen); marks made after it are shown in a distinct style; **Keep** merges them, **Discard** restores the board to the moment the guess began (itself one undo step). Small to build (about half a day with tests): a checkpoint index into the existing undo history plus a snapshot of the edges to color the differences; the board analysis and hints don't change. Design choices: Check and Hint treat guess marks like normal marks (a wrong guess then shows as an error); solving the puzzle during a guess keeps it; a reload mid-guess must not silently accept the guess; keep it a visible layer, not a tool mode (the old "x mode" was removed on purpose). Possible companions: re-dragging a finished tile un-draws it (removes its outline edges that no other finished tile shares); hold-to-rewind on Undo / Ctrl-Z; and an optional "show dead ends" setting that highlights cells with no possible tile or pockets that can't be tiled, so a failed guess is obvious (off by default, since it reveals information).
- **More ways to organize books (later).** The books are one per board size and number of shapes. Possible additions: three-shape books (triples generate fine), board-character themes (wide open vs many blocked cells, narrow corridors), a daily puzzle from a reserved slice of the pools. A puzzle lives in at most one book (`tools/books/collections.json`).
- **Diversity guard in the book builder.** The hunter no longer perturbs stored puzzles by default (`--exploit`), and `node tools/prune_library.mjs --apply` drops near-duplicates from the (local, git-ignored) pools. Still open (more pressing now that each book holds 1000 puzzles, so many more are picked from each pool): make `tools/build_books.mjs` itself skip a puzzle too close (blocked cells differing in under about 8% of the cells) to one already in the book, so bigger books stay varied even when a pool has siblings.
- **More generator speed (optional).** The WASM counter (`tools/wasm`) made generation about 1.6x faster than the cached JS one (about 2x overall since the placement caching); the counter is still about 80% of generation time, so the next steps would be a native addon instead of WASM, a more cache-friendly node layout, or a smarter search (e.g. a cheaper uniqueness test per move). Giants (20x20 and up) need a different method (for example building the board from independently unique regions), not just speed.
- **Sparseness as a quality bar.** The user's priorities are quality, then variety, then total count. The books now prefer sparse puzzles (`sparseShare`) and there are Sparse books (`maxBlocked` 0.17), but the regular books still hold dense puzzles (median 19 to 31% blocked). Decide a hard cap (e.g. a `maxBlocked` for every book) and let the books shrink to what passes; `tools/sparsify_library.mjs` finds few new sparse puzzles beyond what the pools already hold, so supply at the sparse end is mostly fixed by what the generator can reach (6×6 single shapes: only 43 puzzles at or under 15% blocked).
- **Re-evaluate the 17% sparseness cap.** The Sparse books (one and two shapes) take puzzles with at most 17% blocked cells (`maxBlocked` in `tools/books/collections.json`). Revisit it: a tighter cap (say 12%) would give even more L-panel-like puzzles (several thousand pair puzzles at 8×8 and 10×10 pass 12%, only a few hundred single-shape 6×6 ones), a looser one grows the books. Also decide whether the regular books (now under "More books") should shrink to a cap of their own, and whether the shape-by-shape tour (now only in the secondary `one-6`) should move to the default `sparse-6` book.
