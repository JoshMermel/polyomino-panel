- **Rectangular boards, trim wasted black edges.** A surprisingly large number of boards have a whole row or column (sometimes two edges) of black squares. Things to sort out: the puzzle hash includes the grid, so saved progress for trimmed puzzles would restart; books group puzzles by board size, which should become the trimmed size (or area); the solution string and rating don't change (blocked border cells play no part).
- **Guess layer (a checkpoint), for solving by short guess chains.** After a failed guess there can be 10 to 40 marks to erase, and Undo takes a tap per step. About 14% of the published puzzles need chains or more, and roughly a quarter to a third need a trial of some kind, mostly in the Hard books. Idea: a **Guess** toggle next to Undo starts a tentative layer (like using a different pen); marks made after it are shown in a distinct style; **Keep** merges them, **Discard** restores the board to the moment the guess began (itself one undo step). Small to build (about half a day with tests): a checkpoint index into the existing undo history plus a snapshot of the edges to color the differences; the board analysis and hints don't change. Design choices: Check and Hint treat guess marks like normal marks (a wrong guess then shows as an error); solving the puzzle during a guess keeps it; a reload mid-guess must not silently accept the guess; keep it a visible layer, not a tool mode (the old "x mode" was removed on purpose). Possible companions: re-dragging a finished tile un-draws it (removes its outline edges that no other finished tile shares); hold-to-rewind on Undo / Ctrl-Z; and an optional "show dead ends" setting that highlights cells with no possible tile or pockets that can't be tiled, so a failed guess is obvious (off by default, since it reveals information).

- **A "try a line" rule (hint idea). DONE as the Medium rule `tryLine` (2026-11-05); the notes below are the original analysis.** For each undrawn edge, suppose a line were drawn there; if some cell is then left with no consistent tile placement (no lookahead), the edge must be an x. Finding (experiment on a sample of 180 Hard puzzles from the 8x8 books): this is the cross half of the edge rules (a cell whose every placement has that edge inside), so it adds no deductive power; it is a different framing and tier. Treating wide (four or more placements) cross deductions as Medium re-bins about 26% of the sampled Hard puzzles (46 of 180) to Medium, because their only Hard step was such a cross. To do: decide whether the dead cell must be near the edge, split `edgesWide` into a Medium "try a line" rule (crosses) and a Hard edge rule (lines), rescore (`tools/rescore_library.mjs`), rebuild, and make the hint explain it as "a line here would leave this cell with no tile".
- **More deduction rules for the scorer and the hints.** PARTLY DONE (2026-11-05): the chain rule now only applies to cells with two possible tiles, and a new Hard rule `eliminate` (drop the tiles that fail in one step, let the survivors vote on edges) replaced it for cells with three or more; hint wording is in `js/main.js`. Still open: puzzles that need neither remain "no logical hints" (in the books: about 2 to 3% of the one-shape books, 15 to 25% of the pair books). The books now cap their share of the Hard tier at 20% (`maxBeyondHints` in `tools/books/collections.json`); revisit the cap, or find more rules (see the original experiment). Original experiment on the same sample: 53 of the 180 Hard puzzles (29%) are "no logical hints" (`unsolved`). A parity version of the lookahead (already in `js/logic.js` as `LOOKAHEAD_PARITY`, not in the rule table) solved none of them. Two stronger rules solved 52 of the 53: (1) a chain whose hypothesis is propagated with forced tiles AND edge deductions, not just forced tiles (38), and (2) "eliminate, then edges": drop the placements that fail under hypothesis, then let the survivors vote on edges even when more than one survives (14 more). So `unsolved` mostly means the rule set is weak, not that the puzzles need guessing. Both are heavier for a player to follow (a hypothesis with a chain of edge deductions), so they belong in a new top tier or at the end of Hard, with the hint explaining the chain. To do: implement them in `js/logic.js` and `js/rating.js` with soundness tests, check run time (the experiment took minutes for about 50 puzzles), decide tiers and weights, rescore and rebuild.
