# Polyomino Panel

Prototype of **L-panel** (invented by Naoki Inaba, as presented in Alex Bellos' *Puzzle Ninja*), generalized to any polyomino tile.
All puzzles are generated by this project's own generator (every one has a unique solution); none are copied from the book.

**Rules:** draw along the dotted grid lines to cut the white cells into tiles of the shown shape (rotations and reflections allowed). Black cells are blocked and belong to no tile. The tile shape is displayed above the board. A puzzle can also allow **several shapes** (two or more are shown): every tile is then any one of them, used as often as you like. Everything above is the special case of one shape.

## Run

Plain HTML/CSS/JS modules, no build step. Serve the folder with any static server:

```bash
python3 -m http.server 8765
```

then open <http://localhost:8765>. (Opening `index.html` via `file://` won't work: browsers block `fetch` and ES modules there.) It also works as-is on GitHub Pages.

**Links:** the address bar tracks the current puzzle as `?book=<tile>_<n>x<n>&puzzle=<number>` (for example `?book=T_10x10&puzzle=7`; the number is 1-based), so a puzzle can be bookmarked or shared. A link overrides the saved "resume where you left off" position; an unknown book is ignored. Puzzle numbers follow the book's current order, so a link can point at a different puzzle after books are rebuilt.

## Controls

The main view is deliberately sparse: a title bar (⚙ settings, **?** help), the book button (current shape(s) and board size; it opens the book picker) with the puzzle pager, one info line (the tile shape on the left, "Solved!" on the right once the puzzle is done), the action buttons and the board. The rules and controls are in the **?** "How to play" dialog. Summary:

- **Where a press lands decides the gesture** (all deterministic, nothing depends on timing, and it works with mouse, touch and pen):
  - **On an edge:** a tap cycles it empty → line → small ✕ → empty. A press on an edge that moves more than a small slop is not a tap: it becomes a cell drag (below) that starts in the cell the press landed in, so a drag that happens to start on an edge still works.
  - **On a cell:** drag across cells to trace a tile. The cells you cover are previewed (grey while they are not a tile yet; once they form exactly one of the shapes, in any rotation or reflection, in that shape's color); on release, all of that tile's outline lines are drawn and any lines already inside it are erased (✕ marks inside are kept), all as one undo step. Cells that don't form the tile are ignored with a short message. **Dragging over a tile that is already outlined erases it:** only the lines that tile alone uses come off (a line shared with a finished neighbouring tile stays, one shared with an unfinished or wrongly shaped region goes; ✕ marks stay), as one undo step.
  - **Right-click** an edge toggles a ✕ directly.
  - A whole gesture is one undo step; **Esc** cancels a drag in progress. Touch drags on the board don't scroll the page.
- Edges next to a black cell or the outer border are implicit walls and aren't clickable.
- **← / →** (arrow keys) go to the previous / next puzzle in the book, like star battle; held keys repeat. They are ignored while typing in a field, mid-drag, or with a dialog open.
- Hovering highlights the edge (clickable) or the cell (drag across cells to trace a tile) under the pointer.
- The puzzle's **difficulty tier** is shown as filled and empty stars plus a word (Beginner, Medium, Hard, Expert); hover for what it means. Expert is the puzzles the hint rules cannot finish on their own; they also say "no logical hints" next to the tier.
- **Correct so far?** compares every line and ✕ with the solution and reports in a toast ("So far so good!" or "N marks are incorrect."). It highlights nothing. Unplaced lines aren't counted against you.
- **Hint** never changes your marks: it highlights things on the board and explains them in a toast (the highlights stay until you change the board or dismiss the toast). It does the first of these that applies, easiest first (these are the same rules the puzzle ratings use, see "Difficulty"):
  1. If any mark is wrong, show the wrong marks in red.
  2. **Forced:** a cell that only one tile can cover: highlight the cell (yellow) and the tile (blue), with dashed ghost lines showing the outline to draw.
  3. **Edges:** lines and ✕ that every possible tile for a cell agrees on (an edge on every tile's outline is a line; an edge inside every tile is a ✕). Shown as dashed ghost lines and ghost ✕ marks.
  4. **Two-way lookahead** (a cell with two possible tiles, one of which leads to a contradiction) and **lookahead on 3+ options** (all but one fail): the survivor's dashed outline. A red "!" marks a cell that *every* failing tile strands, when there is one; otherwise the text says what goes wrong (a cell with nowhere to go, or an enclosed region that can't be tiled).
  5. **Chain:** the same, but a tile only fails after following what it forces for several steps.
  6. Otherwise a peek: an edge that can take a ✕ (the solution has no line there), never inside an already-closed shape. The peeks are ranked by how many logical steps (Beginner and Medium rules) each one sets off once it is marked (`rankPeeks` in `js/rating.js`, limited to about a quarter of a second), ties going to the one nearest the player's last mark.

  **Order:** the hints of one kind come nearest first to where the player last marked (board order before any mark). **Step-by-step hints** (Settings ⚙, on by default) make each hint take two presses: first where to look plus a vague idea why (e.g. "there is only one way to cover the yellow cell"; for a line-or-✕ deduction on one edge, a yellow ? on the edge and "something about the yellow edge is certain"), then the move with the full explanation (mistakes and peeks are shown whole).

  **Cycling:** pressing Hint while a hint is on screen steps to the next hint *of the same kind*, wrapping around. Dismissing a hint (clicking its toast, or clicking empty page away from the board and controls) clears the toast and highlights but keeps your place in the cycle, so the next press shows the same hint again (a hint dismissed at its first step goes on to its second); press again for the next hint. Only a change to the board restarts the cycle. The toast moves to the top of the window when sitting at the bottom would cover the board.
- ⚙ **Settings**: dark mode (defaults to your system theme) and tinting of closed tiles (blue, or the shape's own colour, = valid tile; orange diagonal hatching = enclosed but can't become one: hatched rather than flat, so it can't be mistaken for a shape tint).

**Colours** (soft, from the colour-blind-safe Okabe-Ito set, light and dark): blue, orange, pink and bluish green for up to four shapes, shown as the fill of a finished tile and in the shape pictures in the header (those are outlined in the shape's stronger colour). Hints are a pale yellow cell with grey tiles and dashed lines; wrong marks are orange, and wrong regions are hatched. The lines you draw are dark grey (light grey in dark mode). A solved board gets a green glow outside its frame (a blurred green stroke clipped to the outside of the board's shape; the frame itself keeps its colour). All of it is CSS variables at the top of `style.css`. (An earlier version also gave each shape a fill pattern; it was removed as too busy.)
- Ctrl/⌘-Z undo, Ctrl/⌘-Shift-Z (or Ctrl-Y) redo. Progress is saved in `localStorage`, keyed by a hash of the puzzle (tile, size, grid), so it survives renumbering or reordering a set.
- Settings ⚙ → "Load puzzles from a file" opens any puzzle CSV for the session without touching `data/`.
- On localhost only, a git-ignored `data/tmp.csv` shows up as a "tmp" book (group Scratch) for looking at example puzzles; it is never fetched from the published site and the service worker skips it. Write it with `toCsv`; a position can be shown by listing its marks in the `given` column.
- Settings ⚙ → "Offline play" (off by default) saves the app and every book on the device through a service worker (`sw.js`), so it works with no connection. The app files are refreshed together in the background when you open the page online (an update appears on the following visit); books refresh one by one. Turning it off removes the worker and its caches. `?sw=on` / `?sw=off` flips the setting from the address bar. Tested in `tests/sw.test.mjs` against a fake Cache API and network; browsers that don't allow service workers (or insecure origins) just report that it isn't available.
- Settings ⚙ → "Print puzzles" makes a printable booklet from the current book: pick puzzles (`1-10, 15`, up to 500) and an optional answer key (boards with the tile boundaries drawn bold). Boards are laid out by cell size, not by a fixed count: **Comfortable** gives puzzle cells of about 6 mm (a little less on boards wider than 8 cells, down to 4.5 mm at 16) and answer cells of 3.5 mm, **Compact** 4.5 and 3 mm, and each page carries as many columns and rows as fit the smaller of A4 and Letter (186 x 255 mm minus the header): for 6x6 boards 16 puzzles and 36 answers to a page (Compact 25 and 49), for 8x8 9 and 25, for 10x10 6 and 16, for 16x16 4 and 9 (12x12 boards, which no book has now, would be 6 and 12). A selection with several board sizes uses the largest one's grid. It opens the browser's print dialog, where "Save as PDF" gives a file. Booklet pages are built by `js/print.js` (`layoutFor`, `planPrint`) as plain black-and-white SVG, independent of dark mode, for any grid and any board outline; every page header shows the site's address, and the name and address are real links, so they work in a saved PDF.
- Settings ⚙ → "Clear saves" erases your saved progress on every puzzle (two clicks: it asks "Are you sure?" first). Dark mode and tint settings are kept.

Solved = every region of open cells (cells connected through edges without a line) is a valid tile. Any valid tiling counts, not only the stored solution.

How a hint decides what fits: a placement of the tile covering a cell is *consistent* when no line cuts through it and no ✕ lies on its outline (a ✕ means "same tile on both sides"). Lines and ✕ elsewhere don't affect it. This rule is tested exhaustively in `tests/`.

## Books

There are twenty books: **Small, Medium and Large** for each of five groups (Polyomino, i.e. one tile shape, Polyomino pair and Polyomino triple on 6×6, 8×8 and 10×10 boards; Hexes on hexagons of 4, 5 and 6 cells a side; Triangles by number of cells, 50 to 64, 72 to 100 and 144 to 216), plus an Intro book and four Bonus books (symmetric voids, clues, shapes, giants). There are no 12×12 books: the 16×16 Giants cover big boards. They are opened from the **book picker** (tap the book button under the title; a modal with a close button, like star battle's). Each of those five groups is one row with a Small, Medium and Large button (hover for the real size and how many puzzles you have solved there; a size turns green when it is finished); the four Bonus books are listed under one "Bonus" heading, each row showing how many puzzles you have solved in it. The default is the 6×6 one-shape book. (The picker can also tuck a group under "More books"; none does at the moment. The two non-sparse "Dense" groups were removed on 2026-11-08.)

- **Polyomino triple** (`three-6`, `three-8`, `three-10`): each puzzle uses three shapes (about 300 triples of the single shapes, such as F + P + U pentominoes; any `A+B+C_NxN.csv` pool in `tools/library/` is picked up automatically). 300 puzzles in each, 130 Medium, 120 Hard and 50 Expert (a triple has almost no Beginner puzzles, so these books start at Medium), at most 22% blocked cells.
- **Polyomino** (one tile shape) and **Polyomino pair**: puzzles with very few black cells (the Medium and Hard ones at most 17% of the board, the Beginner and Expert ones at most 25%, since those need more blocked cells; boards 6×6, 8×8 and 10×10), the look of the original L-panel puzzles in the magazine that inspired this game. Every tile is the same shape, or either of two shapes (about 170 pairs, such as L + T, V tromino + I tromino or F + P pentominoes). The 6×6 books have 250 puzzles and the others 500, and every book has about the same share of each tier: 10% Beginner, 45% Medium, 32% Hard, 12.5% Expert (6×6 single shapes have too few Expert puzzles, 16 of 250). These are built first, so they get first pick of the sparse puzzles (ids `one-6` ... `two-10`).
- **Intro**: a gentle start. 100 of the easiest puzzles (Beginner, score 7 or less), one shape each, on 4×4, 5×5 and 6×6 boards, smallest boards first. Built from small pools (`recipes.json` `extraSizes`; fill them with `node tools/fill_library.mjs --tile L --size 4`).
- **Bonus: symmetric voids** (`bonus-voids`, 400 puzzles; formerly an Experiments book): the black cells are symmetric (a 180° or 90° rotation, a left-right or diagonal mirror, or a combination) and nothing is pre-drawn; the tiling is unique on its own. Tiles may sit in every orientation, so a symmetric board with one solution has a symmetric solution. Few black cells (3 to 16, about 8 on average); one shape or a pair (about 70% / 30% in every tier but Beginner, which has no pairs). `node tools/make_symmetric_puzzles.mjs --mode voids` makes candidates (also with pairs: `--tiles L+T,V3+L,...`) and `node tools/mix_book.mjs` mixes them into the book (`tools/books/sym_voids.csv`).
- **Bonus: clues** (`bonus-clues`, 400 puzzles; the former balanced-clues and lines-and-crosses Experiments books merged into `tools/books/bonus_clues.csv`, sorted by tier and score): few black cells and pre-drawn edges (a line, or a ✕ meaning "same tile"), shown in black like the blocked cells and locked (they can't be erased, and Reset keeps them; the puzzle is marked "pre-drawn clues") that make the tiling unique. Two kinds sit together. *Balanced*: a sparse board (about 7% blocked) that has a handful of tilings (6 to 120) is made unique by 5 or more clues, a mix of lines and ✕ marks, one shape or a pair; `node tools/make_given_puzzles.mjs --balanced 1` (a greedy cover of edges, extra true edges up to `--min-givens`), run with several `--seed`s and merged so no shape dominates a size and tier. *Lines and crosses*: the board is almost empty (only as many black cells as a tiling needs: often none, 4 for the tetrominoes on 6×6 and 10×10) and the clues alone make the tiling unique, a mix of lines and ✕ marks (at least two of each, 4 to about 30 in all); `node tools/make_clue_puzzles.mjs --mode both` (`lines` and `crosses` modes exist too): a random tiling of the minimal-void board is the answer, random true clues that rule out another tiling the search finds are added until it is unique, then every redundant clue is dropped. Shapes that cannot tile a rectangle (F, S, T5, V5, W, X5, Z) only appear in pairs, since alone they need a lot of blocked cells. Both tools try the line pieces (domino, I3, I, I5) rarely and the rigid pentominoes less than the rest (`tools/lib/tile_weights.mjs`), with at most three puzzles of a shape per size and tier (one for a line piece).
- **Bonus: shapes** (`bonus-shapes`, 400 puzzles): boards that aren't plain squares: a square with an outline cut out of it, so the cells outside the shape are not part of the board. 16 board shapes, 25 puzzles each: a plus sign (9×9 and 12×12), an octagon (8×8, 10×10), a diamond (9×9, 11×11), a donut with a square hole that isn't drawn (9×9, 10×10, 11×11), a ring whose hole is blocked cells, an L (8×8, 10×10), a T, an H, a U and a staircase (9×9). 12 single tiles (L, S, T, V3, F5, L5, N5, P5, W5, Y5, U5, Z5; the L tetromino counts double) and 8 pairs (L+T, L+S and V3+L get the most; T+S, F5+P5, N5+W5, L5+Y5, P5+W5), Beginner to Expert. Made by `node tools/make_shaped.mjs --shape plus9 --tile L --count 3` (the generator's `fixed` option keeps the outline blocked; the shapes are in `tools/lib/board_shapes.mjs`) and picked by `node tools/make_shapes_book.mjs <directory of pools>` into `tools/books/shapes.csv`, a source book. Rectangles were tried and dropped as boring. The cells outside a shape are not drawn at all and the frame follows the outline (the optional `outside` CSV column, `x` for an outside cell); blocked cells inside the shape (and the ring's hole) are black as usual. Printing leaves them out too (`js/print.js` draws any board with an outline cell by cell, like hex and triangle boards).
- **Hexes** (`hexes-s`, `hexes-m`, `hexes-l`; 49, 143 and 151 puzzles): the same game on hexagonal cells (see "Grid topologies"), on hexagons with sides of 4, 5 and 6 cells (37, 61 and 91 cells = Small, Medium, Large), one tile each from up to 29 polyhexes (the triangle trihex is the most common; the straight pieces are down-weighted), at most 17% blocked cells (25% for Beginner and Expert). The Small hexagon has few puzzles that meet those caps, so its book is short. Made by `node tools/make_shaped.mjs --shape hexagon5 --tile hex:h4b --from 0.02 --to 0.17 --count 24` for every board and tile (a second, denser pass with the default `--from 0.06`) and picked by `node tools/make_grid_book.mjs hex <directory of pools>` into `tools/books/hexes-{s,m,l}.csv`, source books (each size gets its own tier shares). Hex puzzles are rated by the same rules (so hints work); the parity rule does not exist on hexes.
- **Triangles** (`triangles-s`, `triangles-m`, `triangles-l`; 94, 121 and 151 puzzles): the same game on triangular cells, one tile each from up to 17 polyiamonds of 3 to 6 triangles (the straight strips are down-weighted), at most 17% blocked cells (25% for Beginner and Expert). The sizes go by number of cells: Small (50 to 64: hexagon of side 3, triangle of 8, rhombus of 5), Medium (72 to 100: rhombus of 6, hexagon of 4, triangle of 10), Large (144 to 216: hexagon of 5 and 6, triangle of 12; best on a large screen). Triangle tilings are rigid, so the pools are heavy on Beginner and Medium: Small has no Expert puzzle and Medium one. Made by `node tools/make_shaped.mjs --shape trihexagon4 --tile tri:i5b --from 0.02 --to 0.17 --count 16` for every board and tile (a second pass with `--from 0.0` on the biggest boards for harder puzzles) and picked by `node tools/make_grid_book.mjs tri <directory of pools>` into `tools/books/triangles-{s,m,l}.csv`, source books. The board shapes are in `tools/lib/board_shapes.mjs` (`trihexagonN`, `tritriangleN`, `trirhombusN`, regions of the triangle lattice).
- **Bonus: giants** (`bonus-giants`, 398 puzzles, desktop only in spirit: 16×16 boards are small on a phone): 16×16 boards with one or two tile shapes, Medium and Hard only (no Beginner), at 10 to 20% black cells (26 to 51 of 256). Shapes: F5, L5, N5, P5, W5, Y5, T5 and, less often (rigid, so they give the easy opening), S, T, U5, V5, Z5; pairs of F5, L5, N5, W5, Y5 with each other or with U5, V5, Z5. The local-search generator reaches 16×16 for these; it found nothing for the L tetromino or any L pair, V3, X5, or P5 pairs, and 20×20 only worked for a few shapes (see TODO.md), so the book stays at 16×16. Pools: `node tools/fill_library.mjs --tile L5 --size 16 --target 40 --min-fraction 0.14 --max-fraction 0.20 --attempt-seconds 60` (S, T, U5, V5, Z5 need up to 0.25); `node tools/make_giants.mjs --single 60 --pairs 90 --keep giants.csv` picks the book (`tools/books/giants.csv`, a source book; `--keep` keeps the existing puzzles, `--min-tier` sets the floor, default Medium): shares by shape weight, sparsest first, at most 20% 'no logical hints'. Medium 72, Hard 248, Expert 78; about 30% pairs in Hard and Expert, few in Medium (Medium pairs hardly exist at 16×16). The 297 puzzles come from the existing pools with `node tools/mix_book.mjs` (`--min-tier Medium --max-blocked 0.2 --prefer sparse --fill-tiers Medium`).

Each book runs through all four difficulty tiers (Beginner, Medium, Hard, Expert) and gets harder as it goes: sorted by tier, then by score (with neighbouring puzzles mixed a little so shapes don't repeat). Every puzzle is in at most one book, and has exactly one solution; its white (open) cells form one connected region, with no islands cut off by black cells. The L tetromino, the original L-panel shape, is a favourite: `shapeBoost` in `collections.json` doubles its share (and its pairs') in every book where the pools have enough sparse L puzzles (one-6 has few). The **Browse** button next to the puzzle pager opens a grid of the whole book grouped by tier, with solved puzzles (remembered even after Reset), puzzles in progress and the current one marked, plus a "First unsolved" jump. A row of **shape buttons** (a picture of each piece with how many puzzles use it) filters the grid (and "First unsolved") by shape: pick as many as you like and a puzzle shows if it uses any of them (in a two-shape book, a pair shows if either shape is picked); with none picked every puzzle shows. The picks carry over when you switch books (shapes the new book lacks are ignored). In a two-shape puzzle each shape has its own color: in the pictures above the board, and as the fill of a tile once you close it. Puzzles the hint rules cannot finish on their own say "no logical hints" next to their difficulty.

Books are **built, not hand-made**: huge pools of generated, scored puzzles live in `tools/library/` (not published), and `tools/build_books.mjs` deals them out to the books following `tools/books/collections.json` (see "Building books").

### Difficulty: rules, tiers and score

Each puzzle is solved by a rule-based solver that uses the same deductions as the hints, always applying the easiest applicable rule and never looking at the stored solution. The rules, easiest first, with the tier each belongs to (the table lives in `js/rating.js`, so re-binning a rule or changing a weight is a one-line edit):

| Rule | What it does | Tier | Weight |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `forced` | a cell has exactly one possible tile: outline it | Beginner | 1 |
| `lookahead2` | a cell has two possible tiles and one leads to a contradiction | Medium | 6 |
| `edges` | an edge that every one of two or three possible tiles for a cell agrees on: a line if it is on every tile's outline, a ✕ if it is inside every tile | Medium | 3 |
| `tryLine` | a line on some edge would leave a cell (anywhere) with no possible tile, because every tile that could cover it contains both cells beside the edge: mark the edge with a ✕ | Medium | 4 |
| `pocket` | a line on some edge would wall off an enclosed region (together with the lines already drawn) whose number of cells tiles cannot make up, even though no single cell is stranded: mark the edge with a ✕ | Medium | 5 |
| `lookahead3` | three or more possible tiles; all but one lead to a contradiction | Hard | 16 |
| `edgesWide` | the same edge reasoning over four or more possible tiles | Hard | 8 |
| `eliminate` | a cell has three or more possible tiles; some (not all but one) lead to a contradiction in one step, and the tiles that are left all agree on an edge: draw it (outline of every survivor) or mark a ✕ (inside every survivor) | Hard | 22 |
| `chain` | a cell with just two possible tiles, where one fails only after following its consequences for several steps: forced tiles, and also the edges that two or three candidate tiles agree on and the edges where a line would strand a cell, i.e. the pre-drawn clues and everything they make certain are used too (chains are not used when a cell has more tiles: branching over several tiles and following chains is too much to keep in your head) | Hard | 30 |
| (none) | the rules get stuck ("beyond the hints"; still Hard) | Hard | 40 per undetermined tile |

A **contradiction** is a cell left with no possible tile, or a region walled in by lines whose size is not a multiple of the tile size (the "enclosed region" check), or, inside chains only, a region whose chessboard colours are unbalanced in a way no tiles can produce (a parity test; on the current library it never changes a result, but it is sound and free).

A puzzle's **tier** is the tier of the hardest rule it needed; its **score** is the weighted sum of the deductions used (plus the penalty for undetermined tiles and a small 0 to 30 tie-breaker from the size of the search needed to prove uniqueness, only for puzzles that get stuck, so stuck puzzles differ). Rule, tier and score are stored in each book's `rules`, `tier` and `score` columns but not shown in the app.

How the rules fare on the pools (about 115,000 puzzles): roughly 19% Beginner, 37% Medium, 44% Hard, of which about 4% are beyond the hints. Rigid shapes (the I pieces, T5, U5, V5, Z5, X5) never get past Beginner/Medium; flexible ones fill every tier. The tier boundaries follow the technique, not the score, so they mean the same on every board size (a 10×10 forced-only puzzle scores higher than a 6×6 one but is still Beginner).

## Puzzle CSV format

```
name,rows,cols,tile,grid,solution,rules,tier,score
L_6x6_01,6,6,L,#.....#...#.............#...........,#AAABB#ACC#BDDDCEBDFGCEH#FGEEHFFGGHH,edges,Medium,9
```

- `grid`: `rows*cols` chars, row-major. `#` blocked, `.` open.
- `tile`: a name or an inline pattern. Names: tetrominoes `L I O T S`, `I2` (domino), `I3` and `V3` (trominoes), pentominoes `F5 I5 L5 N5 P5 T5 U5 V5 W5 X5 Y5 Z5`. A pattern is rows of `X` and `.` separated by `/`, e.g. `X./X./XX` (L), `XX/XX/X.` (P pentomino); any 4-connected polyomino works. Optional, default `L`. **Several allowed shapes** are joined with `+`: `L+T`, `I2+V3`, `XX/X.+T`. Each tile of the solution is any one of them (any number of times; the shapes may have different sizes), and the puzzle must have exactly one such tiling. A shape can't be listed twice, even spelled differently.
- `solution`: optional tile label per open cell (`#` for blocked). Used by Correct-so-far and hints; if missing it is computed when the puzzle has a unique solution.
- `rules`, `tier`, `score`: optional difficulty (see above).
- `given`: optional pre-drawn edges, items joined by `;`, each `<L or X>:<h or v>:<row>:<col>` (L = a line, X = a cross; `h` is the edge above cell (row, col), `v` the edge left of it). They are the starting marks (so the ratings and hints use them), are drawn black and locked, and are part of the puzzle's identity (its hash). A puzzle with `given` is unique only together with them (`solveWithGivens`).
- Lines starting with `#` and blank lines are ignored.

## Grid topologies

A puzzle lives on a *topology*: square cells (the original game) or hex cells, read from its `tile` string (`hex:h4b` is a hex tile, anything else is square; every shape in a `+` list carries the prefix, or only the first one does). `js/topology.js` is the one place that knows what a grid looks like:

- **Cells and edges.** A board is `rows` x `cols` cells whatever the grid, cell `(r, c)` is index `r*cols + c`, and the `grid` string says which are open. An edge is `[kind, r, c]`: the side of cell `(r, c)` that faces the neighbour at `(r, c) + kindOffset[kind]`. Square kinds are `h` (above) and `v` (left of a cell); hex kinds `w`, `nw`, `ne`. `dirs` lists a cell's neighbours with the edge between each pair and which side of the cell's polygon it is, so every loop in `logic.js` (regions, outlines, placements, the pocket test) is one loop over `dirs`. Marks are one array per kind (`edges.h`/`edges.v` for squares, so saves and CSVs of square puzzles did not change).
- **Hex coordinates.** Axial: cell `(r, c)` touches `(r, c±1)`, `(r±1, c)`, `(r-1, c+1)` and `(r+1, c-1)`; row `r+1` is drawn half a cell right of row `r`, so a `rows x cols` array is a parallelogram. A hexagonal board of side `n` is the `(2n-1) x (2n-1)` array with the two corners cut off (the `outside` column marks them: the same mechanism as the plus-sign boards). The board is drawn in the box round its in-shape cells.
- **Shapes and symmetries.** Tile patterns (`XX/X.`) are rows of cells in the grid's own coordinates; orientations come from the topology's `rotate` (90 degrees on squares, 60 on hexes) and `reflect`. The hex catalogue (`js/hex_shapes.js`) has every free polyhex of 1 to 5 cells (1, 1, 3, 7, 22); a tile is `hex:h3b` and so on. Differences from squares: no swastika filter, and no parity rule (hexes have no chessboard colouring; `parity` mode falls back to the size test).
- **Geometry.** `polygon`, `center`, `cellAt`, `hit` (an edge's click strip), all in cell units (one cell = 1 wide), so line widths and glows mean the same on every grid. `board.js`, `tile-view.js` and `print.js` draw polygons from these and find the cell or edge under the pointer generically.
- **Triangles.** Rows of triangles: cell `(r, c)` points up when `r + c` is even; an up cell touches left, right and below, a down cell left, right and above (`dirsAt(r, c)`), with edge kinds `w` (left) and `h` (the flat edge above a down cell; `hasEdge` says an up cell has none). A triangle is 1.25 wide. Rotating by 60 degrees turns up triangles into down ones, so rotation and reflection work on a cell's three corners. A tile may only move by an offset `(dr, dc)` with `dr + dc` even (`okShift`, used by `boardPlacements`, the placements through a cell and the generator's random packing; `originShift` is how shapes slide to the origin, in `logic.normalize` and `canonicalGrid`), so every tile keeps pointing the same way. Up and down triangles alternate like a chessboard but a move does not swap them (`flipsColor: false`), so the parity rule is stronger here than on squares. The catalogue (`js/tri_shapes.js`) has every polyiamond of 1 to 6 cells (1, 1, 1, 3, 4, 12): `tri:i4b`.
- **Adding a grid.** Write its entry in `topology.js` (neighbours, edge kinds, the 12 or 8 symmetries, distance, geometry), its shape catalogue, and a board shape in `tools/lib/board_shapes.mjs`; the hooks above cover grids that are not a plain lattice. Then `tests/hex.test.mjs` and `tests/tri.test.mjs` are the template: tables agree with each other, symmetries keep neighbours, the solver counts known tilings.

## Building books

```
generate + score  ──►  pool (tools/library/<shape>_<n>x<n>.csv)  ──►  recipe + build_books  ──►  tools/books_staging/  ──►  --promote  ──►  data/ + manifest.json
 fill_library.mjs        hundreds of scored puzzles, not published                              report to review
```

1. **Fill the pools.** `node tools/fill_all.mjs --target 400` fills every combination in `recipes.json` (19 single shapes and every pair × 3 sizes; `node tools/overnight.mjs` does the pairs in resumable batches) in parallel; `node tools/fill_library.mjs --tile P5 --size 8 --target 600` does one. The number of blocked cells is the main difficulty knob (more blocked cells = easier), so the filler sweeps it: it finds the smallest count the generator can handle for that shape and size, then spreads the puzzles evenly over that count and every larger one (up to 45% of the board). Every puzzle is scored as it is added, rotated/mirrored duplicates are skipped, and re-running tops the pool up.
2. **Build.** `node tools/build_books.mjs` stages every book in `tools/books_staging/` and prints a report (tier mix, number of shape sets, share of Hard that is beyond the hints, score range per tier); `--promote` replaces the books in `data/` and rewrites `manifest.json`. Add `--seed N` for a different (still deterministic) pick; `--list` shows how many puzzles each book can draw from per tier.
3. **How a book is picked.** `tools/books/collections.json` sets the board sizes, the number of shapes per group and the book size (`perBook`; a group may give a number or a size-to-number map). For each book the size is split over the tiers by `tierWeights` (40 Beginner, 180 Medium, 130 Hard, 50 Expert, read as shares; a group may set its own, as Polyomino triple does, and `tierMaxBlocked` gives the Beginner and Expert tiers a looser blocked-cell cap; Expert is never more than its share, so every book has the same share; a tier with too few puzzles gives up what it can't fill and the others share it; the tour counts toward Beginner), each tier's share is split over the shapes or pairs that have puzzles of that tier in proportion to their weights (`shapeWeights` in `collections.json`: 1 unless listed, so evenly; the eight boring shapes weigh 0.33, but every shape with puzzles in a tier gets at least one), and within a shape puzzles are taken at evenly spaced *ranks* of their score among the sparsest half (`sparseShare`: the half with the fewest blocked cells, as long as that leaves three times the number needed; a group with `maxBlocked`, the Sparse books, only takes puzzles under that fraction and is built before the others), from the easiest to the hardest (`pickQuantiles` in `tools/lib/pick.mjs`), so a book follows the pool's own spread of difficulty. A tier that comes up short (the line-piece cap leaves Beginner small, or a sibling was dropped) is topped up from Medium and Hard with non-line puzzles, so every main book has exactly its size. A book keeps only one of a family of siblings (same tile spec and either `minBoardDistance`, 0.08: blocked cells differing in at most 8% of the board's cells after any rotation or reflection, or `sharedTileShare`: at least 85% (6×6), 60% (8×8) or 50% (10×10) of the tiles in exactly the same cells after any rotation or reflection; two Beginner puzzles are exempt from the second test). The book is then sorted by tier, then score, and reshuffled locally and deterministically (`spreadByTier` in `tools/lib/pick.mjs`: the next puzzle is the first of the next 40 whose shape or pair hasn't appeared in the last 4), so the same shape doesn't come up again and again; a puzzle moves only a little and stays in its tier. The hardest few puzzles of a book can still share a shape when only that shape makes such puzzles. The `tour` entry puts a few easy puzzles of each shape in turn at the start of one book. Books are made in order and a puzzle used by one book is never offered to a later one. A rebuild is **stable**: every puzzle already in `data/` stays and only the gaps in the shares are filled (`pickStable`), so adding a shape or raising `perBook` only adds puzzles and players keep their progress; `perBook` is therefore a floor. `--trim` additionally trims shapes whose weight is below 1 down to their share, and a tier's line-piece puzzles down to `maxLineShare` of the tier (use it after lowering a weight; it drops published puzzles of those shapes only), and `--fresh` rebuilds everything from scratch (the way to trim a book or drop a shape). Ties are broken by a hash of the puzzle's content salted with the seed (as in star battle's builder), so they don't depend on names, pool order or pool size; a puzzle published in one book is never taken by another (nor a board an Experiments book holds, up to rotation and mirror); on sparse boards the line pieces (domino, I tromino, I tetromino, I pentomino) are almost the only shapes with Beginner puzzles, so a tier holds at most `maxLineShare` (30%) of them and the local reshuffle treats them as one kind, so a book's opening is not a run of them; and a rebuild only ever adds (published puzzles stay, even past the sparseness slice or the "no logical hints" cap), so rebuilding twice in a row changes nothing the second time.
4. **Rescoring.** After adding a rule or changing weights in `js/rating.js`, run `node tools/rescore_library.mjs` (re-rates every pool in parallel; all 57 pools take about 20 seconds) and rebuild the books. Re-binning a rule into a different tier (changing its `tier` in the rule table) needs a rescore too, since the stored `tier` column is derived from it. To re-rate the published books *without* swapping puzzles (rebuilding picks by score rank, so even small score changes replace many puzzles and restart players' progress on them), run `node tools/rerate_books.mjs`: it keeps each book's puzzles, updates their rules/tier/score and re-sorts.

5. **Hunting for hard puzzles.** Plain sampling finds few puzzles at the top of the rule ladder, so `node tools/hunt_all.mjs --hours 3` (default targets: puzzles whose hardest rule is `eliminate`, `chain` or `unsolved`; `--want` takes tier names or rule ids, e.g. `--want Beginner,Medium` for easy puzzles) runs `tools/hunt_library.mjs` on every combination (`node tools/hunt_library.mjs --tile L --size 8 --minutes 30` does one). It stores only puzzles that need the Hard/Expert rules or defeat them, by *exploring* (fresh sampling, with the blocked-cell count chosen by running yields). An optional *exploit* mode (`--exploit 0.6`: take a hard puzzle, perturb it with the generator's window moves, keep neighbours that are still uniquely solvable) finds many more per minute but only near-duplicate siblings, so it is off by default. A diversity guard skips near-duplicates (fewer than 4 cells different), and combinations with nothing above Medium are written off after a short probe.
6. **Swastikas.** A solution whose drawn tile boundaries contain star battle's swastika pattern (the 8-segment pinwheel of a cross with four hooks, in any rotation or mirror, inside some 4x4 window) is never published: `generatePuzzle` rejects them (`allowSwastika: true` to keep them), the hunter skips them, and `build_books` filters pools. The outline of the black squares counts as drawn boundary (an edge between an open cell and a blocked one is a line of the pattern; an edge between two blocked cells is not), because that is what a player sees. About 6% of puzzles contained one when only tile boundaries counted, about a third do with the black outlines.
7. **Connected boards.** A puzzle whose white cells are split into islands by black cells is never published: `generatePuzzle` rejects it (`allowDisconnected: true` to keep it), the hunter skips it, and `build_books` filters pools (`openCellsConnected` in `js/logic.js`). About 18% of the earlier puzzles had islands.

`tools/books/recipes.json` lists the `shapes` (single shapes and pairs like `L+T`) and `sizes` whose pools the filler, hunter and book builder use; `tools/books/collections.json` lists the books.

`tools/generate.mjs` is the general generator: `--tile`, `--size` (or `--rows/--cols`), `--count`, `--holes` (default automatic), `--seed`, `--out`. Every puzzle it makes has exactly one solution (re-checked with the plain solver), and its output is rated and sorted by rules needed.

How generation works: a local search over (blocked cells, tiling) pairs where the tiling is always a valid solution. Each move re-randomizes a small window of nearby tiles and blocked cells, and is kept if the puzzle's total solution count (counted with DLX) doesn't increase. It stops when the count is 1. Typical times for L: 6×6 about 30 ms, 8×8 under 0.2 s, 10×10 under about 1 s per puzzle.

## Solver

Tiling search in `js/logic.js` is Knuth's Algorithm X with dancing links, always branching on the cell with the fewest remaining placements. `tools/bench_solvers.mjs` compares it with the original first-empty-cell backtracker (kept in `tests/reference_solver.mjs`, also used to cross-check results) and with two array-based variants of the same fewest-options idea. Speed-ups over the original (wall time):

| Workload | scan | counters | dlx | fewer nodes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Uniqueness check of the 72 L puzzles | 4.0× | 5.1× | 6.2× | 8.1× |
| Generator-style counting, 10×10 L (cap 40) | 8.6× | 10.4× | 18× | 12× |
| 12×12 L (cap 40) | 2.9× | 4.7× | 10× | 5.4× |
| P pentomino, 10×10 (cap 40) | 6.1× | 6.2× | 10× | 10× |
| Enumerate 6×8 empty board (cap 100000) | 5.5× | 4.6× | 11× | 4.5× |

Run the script for fresh numbers on your machine.

The deduction rules are fast: rating all the published puzzles (about 3,400 now) takes about 6 seconds (3.5 ms each), and the slowest hint (a stuck position where every rule is tried) takes about a quarter of a second. Each cell's possible placements are precomputed once per puzzle, which sped everything up about 3x. A profile of a rating pass shows 28% of the time in the placement check, so an incrementally maintained per-cell cache could save at most about a quarter of the time: not worth the complexity unless the rules get much more expensive.

## Data

`data/` holds the published books (`<shape>_<n>x<n>.csv`, e.g. `P5_8x8.csv`) and `manifest.json` (the book list and the default book). All of it is generated, so there is no third-party puzzle content. `tools/library/` and `tools/books_staging/` are scratch (gitignored).
